iPrice2026

iPrice is the desktop application the staff at DE.MI Europe use to run the whole price survey cycle: they prepare the baskets of products to survey, plan the store visits, import the prices collected by the field surveyors, check and correct them, and finally publish them to clients, who browse them in WebPrice. It works every day on tables with hundreds of thousands of rows, with the same freedom as an Excel sheet.

The Challenge

Behind every survey a client sees in WebPrice there is a lot of back-office work: a product catalogue of almost 800,000 items, thousands of surveys, EAN and client codes to keep aligned, and prices to verify one by one before they are published. The previous system made this work slow and cumbersome, especially for bulk operations.

Goals

  • Handle the whole survey cycle in a single application, from the product basket to publication
  • Open, filter and edit tables of hundreds of thousands of records without slowdowns
  • Classify the whole product catalogue by the ECR standard and keep it consistent over time
  • Offer tools that work like Excel: copy and paste between cells, find and replace, grouping, per-column filters and export
  • Automatically catch errors (duplicates, inconsistent promotions, abnormal prices) before they reach the client
  • Roll out updates to every workstation without manual reinstalls

Key Challenges

The price grid of a survey places next to each product the columns of every surveyed store (price, promotion, original price), with headers showing the retailer, address, date and index. Keeping it smooth across hundreds of rows and dozens of columns, with in-place editing and colours that flag anomalies, together with the consistency checks run on the data before publication, were the hardest technical parts.

The Solution

I designed and built iPrice as a Windows Forms application on .NET Framework, using the DevExpress controls: ribbon, side navigation, multiple tabs and grids with filters, grouping and in-place editing. The data lives in SQL Server and is read and written through a data access library shared with the WebPrice backend.

Surveys and Prices

A survey groups the store visits made for one client over a given period. The list can be grouped by year and shows, under each survey, the individual store visits with store, date, handheld terminal and service type. From here visits can be merged, duplicated or imported.

Opening a survey leads to the price grid: one row per product and, for each store, price, promotion and original price. Price colours show the change from the previous survey, duplicated client codes and descriptions are highlighted, and the ribbon turns on advanced filters, promotions and percentage views, and generates the file for the client. A survey stays private until it has been checked. Once it is made public it becomes read-only and shows up in WebPrice.

Product Baskets

A basket is the list of products the surveyors have to look for on the shelves. It is imported from the client's file, fully, partially or as a replacement, and its items are linked to the product catalogue. Baskets can be cloned to prepare the next campaign and archived when no longer needed; the item list also shows the buyer responsible for each product.

Baskets: list with number of items and products linked to the catalogue

Master Data

Products, families, ECR categories, stores, suppliers and clients each have their own master data screen, all built on the same grid: per-column filters, grouping by dragging a header, search, copy and paste between cells and Excel export. The product catalogue holds almost 800,000 items, with highlighted EAN codes and the option to merge duplicates.

Product catalogue: almost 800,000 items in a single grid

ECR Classification

ECR is the standard product classification used in grocery retail: a five-level tree running from the department (for example Household care) down to the finest segment (Hand laundry – Liquid), with more than 10,000 entries. Each level adds two digits to the code, so a category's code gives its whole path. Moving the entire product catalogue onto this classification, and keeping it aligned over time, was one of the largest pieces of work in the project.

  • ECR master data: each entry shows its level, code and full path. To create a new one you just type the code: the application finds the parent category by itself, builds the full description as you type and blocks codes that already exist
  • Bulk import: a file of EANs and ECR codes assigns the category to thousands of products at once. Rows that cannot be matched go into a separate file, ready to be fixed and imported again
  • Five levels in the grid: in the product and price grids each ECR level has its own column, filterable and editable from a drop-down. The ribbon chooses which levels to show, and the choice is remembered
  • Consistency check: the Family ≠ ECR filter shows only the products whose internal family does not match their ECR category, or that have neither, so inconsistencies are found and fixed straight away

The same tree feeds WebPrice, where each client analyses price indices by ECR category and each user sees only the categories they are responsible for.

Store Visits and Quality Control

Each store visit goes through a sequence of states: created, exported to the surveyor's handheld terminal, imported with the collected prices, and finally confirmed or suspended. The database maintenance section, reserved for administrators, looks for the anomalies that manual checks miss: promotional prices higher than the full price, promotions without a code, prices far off their history. Corrections are made directly in the grid, even on all flagged rows at once.

Technologies Used

Development

iPrice grew alongside the previous system: the client adopted it from the very first versions and gradually asked for every feature of the old program to be brought over, until it replaced it entirely. It has now reached version 2.5 and keeps evolving with frequent releases.

Methodology

  • Frequent releases: small updates agreed with the people who use the application every day, each one described in release notes available from inside the app
  • Automatic updates: on startup the application checks for a new version and installs it by itself, with no manual steps on the workstations
  • Shared data access: SQL queries written with Dapper in a library shared by iPrice and the WebPrice backend, so both applications read the same data with the same rules
  • Reusable components: extensions to the DevExpress controls (grids, editors, bars) that give every screen the same behaviour
