A long-range shooter doesn't take a guess to the field — they take a verified ballistic table. GS Ballistics exists for that one purpose: given caliber, bullet weight and ballistic coefficient, muzzle velocity and scope height, it computes the elevation (in MOA or mrad) to dial in at every distance in your range, and logs the elevation you actually verified on target right next to the calculated one, distance by distance.

The ballistics

Within a few hundred meters, almost any pre-made table will do. Past a thousand, the trajectory curves faster and faster, and a small error in any single input — velocity, ballistic coefficient, atmospheric conditions — becomes a real elevation error on target: only an engine that genuinely integrates the trajectory, fed with data that's actually current, gets you on target within the first few rounds. It comes down to good input, good output: no software, however sophisticated, makes a sloppy input reliable. GS Ballistics is built around taking both the calculation and the data feeding it seriously.

The engine is a point-mass solver that numerically integrates air drag and gravity along the whole trajectory, using the standard G1 and G7 drag tables (the historical Ingalls/McCoy curve) indexed by Mach number — not a calculator wheel or a simplified formula. Air density and speed of sound are derived in real time from temperature and pressure you actually measured, instead of always assuming standard atmosphere.

It includes a public library of 200+ bullets (factory data from major manufacturers), multiple weapon and range profiles, and for every weapon/distance combination it logs the field-verified correction right next to the calculated one — a digital data book always at hand.

The engineering

Kotlin and Jetpack Compose (Material 3), with a vertical-slice architecture: the ballistic engine lives in its own pure Kotlin module, separate from the Android app, with its own suite of automated regression tests (JUnit) that check the calculated elevation against known real-world cases — it's not enough for the code to compile, it has to keep returning the right numbers after every change. SOLID principles, CI/CD via GitHub Actions for signed builds and automatic distribution on every push.

The interface also adapts to larger screens: on a tablet, rotating to landscape switches to a side-by-side layout that makes use of the extra space instead of just stretching the phone layout. Data backup and export, and full Italian/English bilingual support.
